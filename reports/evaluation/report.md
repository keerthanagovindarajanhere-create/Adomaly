## 1. Matching rule and evaluation unit
- Evaluation unit: `(campaign_id, metric, date)`.
- An incident's `affected_metric` is mapped to a detector metric: `spend->spend`, `conversions->conversions`, `ctr->ctr`, `conversion_rate->conversions` (the same mapping already used by `tests/test_detection.py::test_dev_benchmark_incidents_are_flagged`).
- A record is **flagged** if its route is `Alert` or `Human Review` (both reach a human; `No Alert` does not).
- A record **detects** an incident if it is flagged, its metric matches the incident's mapped metric, its campaign matches, and its date falls inside `[start_date, end_date]`.

## 7. Assumptions and limitations
- Detector thresholds are the shipped defaults; this evaluation does not tune them.
- Metrics are computed only over `metric in {spend, conversions, ctr}`; `impressions`, `clicks`, `cvr` and `revenue` flags are not scored against any label.
- `conversion_rate` incidents (gradual_degradation) are matched on the `conversions` metric, not `cvr`, for consistency with the project's own existing test (`test_dev_benchmark_incidents_are_flagged`); a `cvr` flag on the same incident is not separately credited or penalized.
- Episode objects aren't period-tagged in `src/episodes.py`; the EVAL-split fatigue numbers approximate this by filtering episodes whose `end_date` falls in or after the EVAL period, and by scoring fatigue over EVAL-period records directly for the primary record-count figures.
- Detection delay is measured in calendar days from an incident's documented `start_date`, not from when the detector had enough history — an incident that starts before a campaign has `min_history` days of data could show as 'not detected' for a reason unrelated to detector sensitivity. None of the EVAL incidents are this early in their campaign's history, but this is a theoretical gap in the method.
- The synthetic benchmark is a designed scenario set, not a sample of real campaign incidents; these numbers describe behaviour on that benchmark, not real-world performance, exactly as `app.py`'s own About tab already states.

## 8. Controlled comparison — synthetic EVAL split

Three systems scored on the same records, labels and matching rule as sections 1-3:

- **Fixed threshold**: flag when a metric moves by at least **90%** vs the previous day (src/baselines.py). The threshold was chosen by DEV-only F1 over a 20-90% grid (`fixed_threshold_grid.csv`); every flag is a notification.
- **Adaptive detector**: the Adomaly detector's flags, but every flag is notified (no evidence routing, no episode grouping).
- **Full Adomaly**: the shipped pipeline output (routing + episodes + escalation).

An incident counts as **notified** only if a `new` or `escalation` notification lands on its mapped metric inside its window; suppressed flags do not count.
